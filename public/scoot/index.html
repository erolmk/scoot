SCootAI@USC 2026 · working prototype

Rent a scooter from a student in your building.

SCoot lets USC students rent out their scooters and bikes to other students. You pick one up at the owner’s dorm rack, ride it, and bring it back to the same rack. The owner earns money while they’re in class.

How a rental works

  1. Book. The app lists the scooters and bikes parked at the rack outside your building. Each shows the owner’s price and curfew (the time it must be back). You book one and have 10 minutes to reach it.
  2. Pick up. At the rack you take two photos: the battery display and the whole scooter. The AI reads the battery % and keeps the “before” photo. Then your phone opens the lock.
  3. Ride. Go to class. While you’re inside, lock it at any campus rack; only you can unlock it. The app shows the cost so far and the battery left.
  4. Return. Bring it back to the same rack before the curfew. You take one photo of it locked. The AI checks the parking and the lock and compares it with the pickup photo for new damage. Close the lock and the rental ends.

What it costs

Each owner sets three prices. A typical owner charges:

First 2 hours and 2 miles$5
Each extra hour, and each extra mile$2
Back after the curfew$10, once

Parkside to the Village gym and back (1.4 miles, 1½ hours) is $5. Riding to a 4-hour lecture and back is $11. Electric rides also need a $25 card hold, released after the return.

What owners do

  • Get a smart padlock and a hardened chain ($86) at no cost. There is no GPS tracker.
  • Pick the days, hours and prices it can be rented. Charge it themselves.
  • Keep 70% of each fare. Another 15% pays off their lock; once it’s paid off, they keep 85%.
  • See who has it and when it’s due back, not where the renter parked.

Logistics

Why round trip?
The scooter always ends up back at the owner’s rack, so nobody has to go find it.
How does the lock open?
The owner’s lock is a TTLock smart padlock. During the rental, the renter’s phone gets a key that expires at the curfew.
Who is responsible during the ride?
The renter, a signed-in USC student. Their phone records its location at every unlock and lock (the ride log).
Battery dies?
The renter kicks it home without the motor, or walks it if that isn’t safe.
Damage, theft or a crash?
The renter reports it in the app (for theft, they also call DPS). AI-flagged damage keeps the scooter off the app until a person checks it.

What the AI does

Four checks, all from phone photos: the battery %, parking and lock, new damage since pickup, and the expiry date on a driver’s license (e-scooters only). The AI never approves a rental or charges anyone. Unclear photos go to a person.

What’s real in this prototype

Real: the whole app and the AI photo checks. Simulated: USC sign-in, payments and the lock. To try a full rental: sign in, book Big Red, and use the demo photos at each step.
