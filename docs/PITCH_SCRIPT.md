# SCoot: 3-minute pitch script

One block per slide, in order. Roughly 2.5 minutes at a normal pace.
Total: 475 words.

## Slide 1: cover (25 words)
SCoot lets a USC student borrow another student’s scooter or bike from the rack outside their dorm, and bring it back to the same rack.

## Slide 2: lime (49 words)
Why not just use Lime? On Lime’s map, USC is a red zone, so Lime scooters pile up at the gates. Students posted these on r/USC in September. If you need a ride on campus, you buy a scooter or you walk. SCoot uses the scooters students already own.

## Slide 3: problem (59 words)
Scooters at USC live on outdoor racks, and they get stolen: DPS logged four taken from campus racks in one evening on February 5, 2025. The ones that survive sit unused for hours while their owners are in class. And students who only need one now and then can’t justify spending three hundred dollars or more, so they walk.

## Slide 4: deal (48 words)
So we start with the owner. They get a smart padlock and a hardened chain on free loan, eighty-six dollars per scooter, plus help registering with DPS. There’s no tracker: the renter’s phone logs where the lock was opened and closed. Owners keep 70 percent of every fare.

## Slide 5: how (69 words)
Every rental is a round trip. We first tried one-way rentals, but scooters would drift across campus and owners would have to find them. Now you pick it up at the owner’s rack, lock it at any rack during class, and return it to the same rack. Five dollars covers two hours and two miles, then two dollars per extra hour or mile. Owners can set their own prices.

## Slide 6: app (36 words)
This is a working prototype you can try now. The photo checks call a real AI model; the accounts, payments and lock are simulated. Owners see the curfew and their earnings, not where the renter parked.

## Slide 7: ai (65 words)
The AI does four jobs from photos. Scooter brands show battery differently and none connect to us, so it reads the display, and the app tracks the battery from that reading and the miles you ride. It checks the parking and the lock, flags new damage against the pickup photo for a person to judge, and reads a license’s type and expiry before scooter rides.

## Slide 8: security (39 words)
We attacked our own AI. A note in the photo said the battery was 100 percent and to approve. It still read the real 12 percent and flagged the note. The AI never approves a rental or charges anyone.

## Slide 9: usc (60 words)
For USC to adopt this, each office has one job, and we still need an operator to run daily support and inspections. We’re proposing a six-week pilot at IRC with five vehicles. It costs about twenty-six hundred dollars if student volunteers run it, or about seventy-nine hundred with paid staff. No real rides happen before insurance, approvals and lock tests.

## Slide 10: next (25 words)
We’re looking for a Student Government sponsor for that pilot. Meanwhile, try the app on your phone at seamengine.com slash scoot. Fight On, Scoot On.
