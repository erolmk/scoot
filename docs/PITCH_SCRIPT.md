# SCoot: 3-minute pitch script

One block per slide, in order. About 3 minutes at a normal pace.
Total: 483 words.

## Slide 1: cover (25 words)
SCoot lets a USC student borrow another student’s scooter or bike from the rack outside their dorm, and bring it back to the same rack.

## Slide 2: lime (49 words)
Why not just use Lime? On Lime’s map, USC is a red zone, so Lime scooters pile up at the gates. Students posted these on r/USC in September. If you need a ride on campus, you buy a scooter or you walk. SCoot uses the scooters students already own.

## Slide 3: problem (49 words)
Scooters at USC live on outdoor racks, and they get stolen: DPS logged four taken in one evening last February. The ones that survive sit unused while their owners are in class. And students who only need one now and then won’t spend three hundred dollars, so they walk.

## Slide 4: demand (40 words)
We talked to twenty students. Six of eight scooter owners said they’d probably rent theirs out, and nine of twelve students without one said they’d probably use it. The other three just don’t make trips long enough to need it.

## Slide 5: deal (52 words)
So we start with the owner. They get an eighty-six dollar smart padlock and hardened chain at no cost, plus help registering with DPS. No tracker: the renter’s phone logs where the lock opened and closed. Owners keep 70 percent; half of our 30 pays off their lock, then they keep 85.

## Slide 6: how (56 words)
Every rental is a round trip: pick it up at the owner’s rack, lock it at any rack during class, and bring it back to the same rack, so scooters never drift across campus. Five dollars covers two hours and two miles, then two dollars per extra hour or mile. Owners can set their own prices.

## Slide 7: app (36 words)
This is a working prototype you can try now. The photo checks call a real AI model; the accounts, payments and lock are simulated. Owners see the curfew and their earnings, not where the renter parked.

## Slide 8: ai (52 words)
The AI does four jobs from photos. It reads the battery display, since no scooter brand connects to us, and tracks the battery from that reading and the miles you ride. It checks parking and the lock, flags new damage for a person to judge, and reads a license’s type and expiry.

## Slide 9: security (39 words)
We attacked our own AI. A note in the photo said the battery was 100 percent and to approve. It still read the real 12 percent and flagged the note. The AI never approves a rental or charges anyone.

## Slide 10: usc (60 words)
For USC to adopt this, each office has one job, and we still need an operator to run daily support and inspections. We’re proposing a six-week pilot at IRC with five vehicles. It costs about twenty-six hundred dollars if student volunteers run it, or about seventy-nine hundred with paid staff. No real rides happen before insurance, approvals and lock tests.

## Slide 11: next (25 words)
We’re looking for a Student Government sponsor for that pilot. Meanwhile, try the app on your phone at seamengine.com slash scoot. Fight On, Scoot On.
