# RideOn: 3-minute pitch script

One block per slide, in order. Roughly 2.5 minutes at a normal pace, leaving time for slide changes.
Total: 394 words.

## Slide 1: cover (25 words)
RideOn lets a USC student borrow another student’s scooter or bike from the rack outside their dorm, and bring it back to the same rack.

## Slide 2: problem (45 words)
USC bans charging and storing e-scooters inside student housing, so they live outside on racks. There, they get stolen: DPS logged four taken from campus racks in one evening on February 5, 2025. And all day they sit unused while their owners are in class.

## Slide 3: deal (50 words)
So we start with the owner. They get a smart padlock, a hardened chain and a hidden AirTag on free loan, about a hundred and seventy dollars per scooter, help registering with DPS, and approved outdoor charging. They keep 70 percent of every fare. The pilot itself needs grant funding.

## Slide 4: how (59 words)
Every rental is a round trip. We first tried one-way rentals, but scooters would drift across campus and owners would have to find them. Now you pick it up at the owner’s rack, lock it at any rack during class, and return it to the same rack. Three dollars for up to two hours, five for up to four.

## Slide 5: app (37 words)
This is a working prototype you can try now. The photo checks call a real AI model; the accounts, payments and lock are simulated. Owners see the return deadline and their earnings, not where the renter parked.

## Slide 6: ai (51 words)
The AI does four jobs from photos. Scooter brands show battery differently and none connect to us, so it reads the display. It checks the parking and the lock, flags new damage against the pickup photo for a person to judge, and reads a license’s type and expiry before scooter rides.

## Slide 7: security (39 words)
We attacked our own AI. A note in the photo said the battery was 100 percent and to approve. It still read the real 12 percent and flagged the note. The AI never approves a rental or charges anyone.

## Slide 8: usc (63 words)
For USC to adopt this, each office has one job, and we still need an operator to run daily support, inspections and charging. We’re proposing a six-week pilot at IRC with five vehicles. It costs about forty-seven hundred dollars if student volunteers run it, or about twelve thousand seven hundred with paid staff. No real rides happen before insurance, approvals and lock tests.

## Slide 9: next (25 words)
We’re looking for a Student Government sponsor for that pilot. Meanwhile, try the app on your phone at seamengine.com slash rideon. Fight On, Ride On.
